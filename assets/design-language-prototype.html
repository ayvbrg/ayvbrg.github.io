Teaching a diffusion model to ink like a human

devlogexperiment

There is a particular arrogance in asking a model that has only ever seen pixels to understand the pressure of a nib.

Ink is not colour. It is decision. A human inker commits: the line thickens where the form turns toward you and starves where it turns away. That commitment is the whole language, and it was the first thing the model refused to learn.

What the model missed

What broke first was line weight. The model produced strokes of perfectly uniform width, technically correct and completely dead. It had learned the shape of ink without the physics of it, so I added weight_variance(pred) to the loss:

Note

The coefficient is illustrative. The useful change came from the examples, not from finding a magic constant.

The variance term was not the answer, but it was the first time the output looked like a decision instead of a trace. The next experiments stayed deliberately small:

  • compare pressure at matching contour points;
  • penalise repeated line widths;
  • inspect every batch before changing the objective.

The model did not need a better description of ink. It needed better evidence of how a line is made.

Three weeks later the real fix was duller and more human: a few hundred examples inked by hand, at two in the morning, until the model had something honest to imitate.

FAQ

The personal questions, answered once.

A standing page for the things people tend to ask. It grows as the questions do.

What do you work on? +
AI tooling for comics at Dashtoon by day, and a rotating cast of side experiments by night, mostly generative art and small developer tools. This log is where the notes on both end up.
What do you build with? +
Mostly TypeScript and Python, whichever model is behaving that week, and a stubborn preference for plain CSS. This site is planned on Astro + MDX with content kept in the open.
What is your setup? +
A tiling window manager, a terminal that is open more than it is closed, and a mechanical keyboard that annoys everyone on calls. Nothing exotic, everything kept boring on purpose.
Why "ayvbrg"? +
A handle that stuck. The .log is the point: a place to keep the work in the open, drafts and dead ends included.
How do I reach you? +
Fastest is GitHub or X. For anything longer, the address in the footer reaches me directly.
